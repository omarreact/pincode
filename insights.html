Strategy note

Rebuild or improve?

Start by identifying whether the problem is isolated or systemic. A few slow assets or unclear labels may justify focused improvements. A site with tangled structure, outdated content, fragile code and difficult publishing may cost more to keep patching than to reorganize properly.

The decision should come from constraints and future needs, not from a desire to follow a design trend.

Performance note

Make fast feel fast.

Technical metrics matter, but the sequence in which content appears and becomes usable matters too. Prioritize critical content, avoid unnecessary movement, reduce blocking work and make interactions respond immediately.

A good performance plan combines measurement with actual user experience.

Operations note

Plan for day two.

Before launch, decide who updates the site, who responds to failures, how backups are handled, how dependencies are maintained and how changes are reviewed.

Operational clarity prevents small maintenance tasks from turning into large recovery projects later.
